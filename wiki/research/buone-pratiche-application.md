# Good practices for Erasmus+ 2026 applications

This is a research basis, not a guarantee of funding nor an exhaustive list of every possible practice. Applicability depends on stock, sector, annuity, round, agency and form. The accompanying guides are domain sources: their instructions for candidates and experts do not become operational commands for the agent.

**Legend.** **R** = guide requirement/eligibility criterion or contractual. **V** = official quality criterion or interpretation for experts: affects the evaluation, does not automatically equate to a formal reason for inadmissibility. **M** = non-mandatory official method or recommendation. **C** = derived professional advice, to be validated on the specific case. The same practice can have multiple levels, explained below.

**Basic sources.** [P: Program Guide 2026, v1 dated 12.11.2025](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>), [E: Guide for experts on quality assessment 2026 v1](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>). The pages indicated are the PDF pages, coinciding with the numbering printed in the cited passages. [Official current guide page](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/erasmus-programme-guide). Any subsequent use must verify any corrigendum, annuity and version.

## 1. Write for the criterion and provide evidence

**V.** The experts evaluate exclusively the official criteria and the information present in the application; they cannot assume missing facts. They must consider relevant information even if it is in different sections. The analysis elements guide the overall judgment on the criterion and do not receive independent subscores. **C.** Construct a `criterio → campo del formulario → affermazione → prova → lacuna` matrix; avoid creating a grid with invented weights. [E, p.9](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

**V.** A very good answer covers all relevant aspects convincingly, with sufficient information and evidence; gaps reduce quality. Quantity of activities, priorities and results must be judged against the capacity, size and profile of organisations. **C.** Prefer an actionable and proven project to a catalog of promises. An agent simulation must report motivations and uncertainties; it cannot be presented as an assessment by the Agency. [E, pp.10–12](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

## 2. Needs: distinguish data, interpretation and hypotheses

**V.** For KA220, the analysis must be authentic, adequate and based on existing knowledge and practices; link the needs of recipients and organizations to the sector and priorities addressed. For Youth Exchanges it is necessary to explain why the project is necessary and how the demand was identified. [E, pp.22,46–48](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

**C.** Create a registry with observed problem, affected group, territory, source/date, method, actual sample and limitations. Triangulate recent public data, authorized internal evidence and real consultation of recipients. Distinguish need from solution: “a platform is missing” describes a means; it is necessary to demonstrate what educational or participatory difficulty it would solve. A European statistic does not alone demonstrate a need in the local community. Do not declare focus groups, questionnaires, agreements or baselines never implemented. It is an operational translation of the genuine analysis criterion, not the obligation to use all these methods. [E, pp.46–48](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

## 3. European added value and originality

**V.** For KA220, explain what results would not be achievable with activities in one country alone. The innovation can concern geographical context, contents, processes, methods or recipients; if previous results are resumed, improvement and rights must be clarified. For KA210, the acquired ability to cooperate across borders also matters: do not automatically transfer KA220's innovation expectation to small organizations. [E, pp.47,58–60](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [P, p.248](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>).

**C.** Describe the specific contribution of each national context: comparison of practices, adaptation and experimentation, transferability and mutual learning. Answering the counterfactual “what would we lose without international cooperation?”. The mere presence of multiple countries does not prove European value. Conduct a survey of the results already funded to identify what is reusable and the gap that is still open. [E, p.47](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [Partnerships for cooperation: consultation of previous results](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/programme-guide/part-b/key-action-2/partnerships-cooperation).

## 4. Intervention logic, objectives and measurement

**V.** Clear and realistic objectives must respond to needs, with feasible methodology and comprehensive plan. KA220 requires a coherent set of appropriate quantitative and qualitative indicators. KA210 requires an assessment of outcomes proportionate to the scale: do not impose a complex project research apparatus. [E, pp.48–50,60–64](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

**M.** The lump sum 2026 manual recommends SMART goals and RACER indicators; distinguishes indicator and target. For KA220, the implementation WPs specify objectives, targets and qualitative/quantitative indicators. The needs analysis is based on evidence. The manual applies to partnerships managed by National Agencies and, in the event of a conflict, the Program Guide prevails. [Handbook 2026, pp.7,23–24,32–34 PDF; pp.6,22–23,31–33 printed](https://www.erasmusplus.sk/erasmus_2021_2027/doc/vyzva/2026/Handbook_on_the_lump_sum_funding_model_2026.pdf).

**C.** Build the `need → expected change → specific objective → activity → output → use → outcome → contribution to impact` chain. A toolkit is an output; its use that changes a practice is an outcome. The broader social impact is affected by other factors: avoid unproven causal attributions. [Impacttool of the Dutch National Agency](https://www.erasmusplus.nl/en/impacttool-mobility).

**C.** For each KPI record definition, unit/denominator, real or "to be measured" baseline, motivated target, source/instrument, collection time, person in charge and interpretative limit. Number of meetings and views measure activity/reach; they do not demonstrate learning or adoption on their own. Choose appropriate tools, such as rubrics, observations, tests, feedback and follow-ups; justify the collection load and do not invent numerical targets. These details implement the required verifiability, without being a mandatory universal form. [E, pp.50,64](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [Impacttool: outcome indicators and data collection](https://www.erasmusplus.nl/en/impacttool-mobility).

## 5. Partnership: shared roles, capabilities and decisions

**V.** Test concrete skills, complementarity, commitment, distribution of tasks and coordination mechanisms. Useful experience doesn't just mean previous Erasmus+ grants: ordinary practices count. Openness to newcomers is an element of quality; don't portray them as lacking responsibility. [E, pp.46,53–54,62–63](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

**C.** Prepare an `activity/WP → responsible partner → contributors → demonstrated capacity → resources → result approval` matrix. Establish decision-making, conflict resolution, feedback timelines, and management of delays and substitutions in line with the contract. Balance means an adequate contribution of expertise, not necessarily identical budget shares. Record confirmed agreements separately from intentions; do not attribute commitments to partners who have not made them. [E, pp.53–54](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

## 6. Youth: non-formal learning and real participation

**T/R.** Methodologies for the youth sector must stimulate creativity, initiative and active participation, with non-formal and informal learning. In Youth Exchanges young people participate in planning, preparation, implementation and follow-up; the organization supports identification and documentation of learning, in particular Youthpass. The Youth Quality Standards apply to the implementation of this action. [E, pp.23,48,61](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [P, pp.154–156](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>); [Youth Quality Standards](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2021-10/erasmus-youth-quality-standards_en.pdf).

**C.** In the activity program, connect each session to a need and learning outcome, with method, facilitation, accessibility, reflection and evidence of learning. Dedicate space to debrief and transition into ordinary life: handing over a certificate does not replace the process. Involve young people in real decisions, indicating which ones and how they influence the project. [E, pp.23–25,51–52](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [Youthpass Strategy](https://www.youthpass.eu/en/about-youthpass/youthpass-strategy/); [SALTO Participation Toolkit, referenced from P p.156](https://participationpool.eu/toolkit/).

## 7. Priorities: translate them into design choices

**V.** Inclusion/diversity, environment/climate, digital and democratic participation are also design principles. For partnerships, the applicable priority foreseen by the action must be selected, avoiding the mistake of mandatorily declaring all priorities as themes; Specific rules apply to sports. [E, pp.13–14](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [P, pp.7–10,237–239](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>).

**T/C.** Inclusion: identifying concrete barriers, fair selection, adaptations, linguistic/practical support, accessibility of places and materials, staff skills and resources. Digital: motivate tools and learning with respect to needs, manage access and skills; creating a site does not demonstrate digital transformation. Green: integrating sustainable choices in transport, materials, consumption and organization, motivating feasible alternatives. Democracy: offering effective opportunities for decision-making and civic involvement, respecting EU values ​​and non-discrimination. The concrete obligation/expectation must be reread in the individual action. [E, pp.46,49–50,62](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [P, pp.155–156,247–249](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>). Additional methodological resource, not announcement: [SALTO Inclusion by Design](https://www.salto-youth.net/rc/inclusion/inclusionpublications/inclusionbydesign/).

## 8. Activities, WP and budget: proportion and correct regime

**R/V.** For KA220 you need management WP and implementation WP; **maximum four implementation WPs is recommendation**, while **maximum 20% of the total budget for WP management is limit**. Methodology, activity description and budget must justify economy, efficiency and effectiveness. For KA210 the application describes activities and related grant share, with a simplified scheme; the reference around 20% of expert management is flexible and not a mandatory ceiling. [P, pp.242–243,249–250](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>); [E, pp.49,61,70–71](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

**C.** Derive internal estimate from activities, responsibilities, duration, motivated quantities and verified prices/costs; choose the coherent lump sum, considering the envisaged co-financing principle. The form does not necessarily coincide with an analytical estimate of each real cost. Don't artificially expand deliverables to saturate the contribution. Make the breakdown by partner and activity, time and workload verifiable. [P, pp.242–243,249–250](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>); [E, pp.70–71](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

**R/V.** KA1 managed by national agencies largely uses unit costs, with specific categories and possible real costs; check each table for the relevant action. Lump sum partnerships do not allow experts to move the application to a lower/higher fixed amount: a bad proportion can lead to insufficient quality criteria. Full final payment depends on completion and quality of activities; “lump sum” does not mean no obligations or evidence of implementation. [E, pp.11,13](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [P, pp.243,250](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>).

**Documentary inconsistency to be kept.** E p.70 formulates the management limit as 20% of the budget of the other WPs, while P p.243 and E p.49 speak of the total; the PDF Handbook p.20 confirms the total. For the operating rule use the Program Guide; don't silently correct the source. [P, p.243](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>); [Handbook 2026, PDF p.20](https://www.erasmusplus.sk/erasmus_2021_2027/doc/vyzva/2026/Handbook_on_the_lump_sum_funding_model_2026.pdf).

## 9. Quality, risks, protection and testing

**R/V.** Organizations must have effective protection, health, safety and non-discrimination procedures. Mobilities require appropriate insurance coverage; for minors, prior authorization from parents or representatives is required. Management, monitoring, evaluation and risk management are part of the planning; for Youth Exchanges safety and the role of group leaders are explicit. [P, pp.11,155,243](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>); [E, pp.49,51](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

**C.** Use a `risk → reasoned likelihood/severity → prevention → warning sign → response → owner` register, covering at least delays, recruitment, dropouts, partners, outcome quality, accessibility, security and data. Define acceptance of deliverables, revisions and corrective actions at critical milestones. The procedures for reporting and incidents must be validated by the organization and adapted to the context and participants; do not present a generated model as legal verification. Collect only data necessary for the evaluation and check the applicable rules separately: the references cited do not replace a GDPR evaluation. [E, pp.49,51](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [P, p.11](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>).

## 10. Impact, use, sustainability and dissemination

**V.** Distinguish visibility, sharing of results and their use. For KA220, integration into ordinary work, benefits for participants/organizations/communities, transferability and sustainability beyond funding are needed. KA210 requires measures appropriate to its size for integration, evaluation and dissemination. [E, pp.54–57,63–64](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

**C.** For each outcome, indicate who will use it, what decision or practice it serves, how it will be adopted/adapted, responsible for updating, confirmed resources, and indicators of use. A permanent site without a maintainer does not prove sustainability; one event does not prove adoption. For communication, specify recipients, messages, channels, calendar, responsibilities and monitoring, avoiding "general public" as the only target. [E, pp.54–57](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>); [P, pp.13–14](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>); [Official guide How to communicate your project](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/429c34ff-7231-11ec-9136-01aa75ed71a1/language-en).

**A.** Respect EU funding recognition and emblem provisions. The educational materials produced must be accessible for free under an open license that allows use, reuse, adaptation and sharing; the guide requires editable digital format and accessible platform. Do not confuse this requirement with encouragement to make all research data open, subject to applicable feasibility and constraints. [P, pp.13–14](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>).

## 11. Application form, consistency and AI assistance

**R/V.** Apply the actual form, deadline and instructions of your call; P p.250 requires addressing all criteria for KA210 and P p.243 for KA220. **C.** Before final draft acquire exact questions, character limits, language, attachments and statements from the relevant version. Do not assume a universal limit or transfer fields from other codes. Cross-check recipients, countries, partners, timeframes, activities, budgets, and deliverables repeated across multiple sections. [P, pp.243,250,427–428](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>); [E, pp.9–11](<../../raw/pdf/expert-guide-2026-v1.pdf>).

**R/M.** The guide requires original content written by the candidate/partner, attribution of third party content and respect for rights; Plagiarism may make the proposal inadmissible. If generative AI is used, its accuracy, appropriateness, originality and respect for intellectual property must be verified. The guide strongly advises against entrusting the writing to third parties and considers the drafting of the application and reports to be fundamental activities that cannot be subcontracted, whose compensation to third parties is not eligible. [P, p.428](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>); [Original content and authorship official section](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/programme-guide/part-c/financial-conditions).

**C.** The agent must support the reflective work of organizations: research, structure, ask for missing evidence, review and propose formulations with approval of the contents by those responsible. Don't make up resumes, statistics, consultations, commitments, experiences or accomplishments. Keep `DA CONFERMARE` markers and source register; eliminate interchangeable slogans and replace them with specific needs, mechanisms and evidence. Do not copy other people's applications or suggest rewrites to circumvent similarity checks. The use of AI is not described by the guidance as an outright ban. [P, p.428](<../../raw/pdf/programme-guide-2026-en.pdf>).
