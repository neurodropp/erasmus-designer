# Phase 0 — Idea Lab and preliminary decision

Phase 0 precedes the application and turns an intuition into a verifiable decision: proceed, refine, change direction, or stop. Brainstorming can be free and divergent; final convergence is constrained by current sources, causal coherence, and feasibility. This phase does not assign an official score, forecast funding, or replace full eligibility checks, the award criteria checklist, or the `idea-validation` gate.

Work can begin before a project file exists. Until the programme, action, and sector have been verified, use the temporary space `work/idea-lab/<ID>/` assigned by the workflow, without inventing a `projects/<ID>/`. Create `stages/phase-zero/decision.md` and `decision.json` according to the [decision contract](../wiki/templates/phase-zero-decision-contract.md); promote the project file and pass the product to `idea-validation` only after the route has been verified, the `PROCEED` recommendation has been accepted, and the observed choice is bound to the exact version.

## Principles

1. **Diverge before converging.** At the start, gather formulations, targets, causes, mechanisms, and alternatives without discarding them for premature compliance. Label each item as user-provided data, evidence, hypothesis, or proposal.
2. **Critique the idea; do not defend it.** Also look for alternative explanations, unintended effects, existing solutions, and reasons why the intervention might not work.
3. **Do not invent.** Unobserved consultations, needs, capacity, partners, baselines, and results remain gaps with a verification plan.
4. **Record an observable synthesis.** Preserve options, decision criteria, evidence, limitations, decisions, and actions. Do not save chain of thought or internal dialogue.
5. **Apply proportionality.** Depth, scale, and resources must be consistent with the action, duration, funding scheme, any tier, and plausible capacity.

The gate freezes two complementary records: `brainstorm.md` for free-form discussion and `brainstorm.json` for the minimum verifiable evidence of divergence. The JSON identifies a primary formulation, at least one relevant alternative concerning the problem, target, mechanism, and scale, provenance, and reasons for convergence. If no relevant alternative can be constructed, it requires a localized justification with evidence and an owner. Its `idea_version` matches the version in `decision.json`; neither record contains internal reasoning.

### Collaborative cadence

Phase 0 is a discussion with the user, not an assessment produced in isolation. Work in short cycles: gather the core idea; return multiple formulations and discriminating questions; compare routes, targets, mechanisms, and scales; show weaknesses and ways to strengthen the idea; make a recommendation; record the user's observed choice. Do not infer consent from silence or from the brief alone. The analytical recommendation and observed choice remain distinct; without the latter, the product may be `decision-ready`, but it does not attest to a human decision.

## 0.1 Minimum brief and problem space

Gather, where available:

- desired change and why it matters;
- observed problem, geographic context, and affected groups;
- proposing organisation, role, and already documented capacity;
- hypothesised programme/call, year, sector, country, and competent authority;
- primary and secondary targets, including age/profile/role where relevant;
- hypothesised duration, countries, partners, resources, and order of magnitude;
- constraints, non-negotiable ideas, alternatives, and open questions.

A policy priority does not demonstrate that a problem exists. A desired solution does not demonstrate need. Formulate the problem as an observable negative condition, without defining it only as the absence of the proposed activity.

## 0.2 Divergent brainstorming

Produce at least one primary formulation and relevant alternatives across four axes:

| Axis | Exploration questions | Record to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | What condition needs to change? For whom? Where? Which causes or effects may have been confused? | Alternative formulations, evidence, and hypotheses |
| Target | Who directly experiences the problem? Who enables or hinders change? Who benefits indirectly? | Primary/secondary targets and intended role |
| Mechanism | Why should the activities lead to different use, behaviour, competence, or practice? | Alternative mechanisms and assumptions |
| Scale | What is a credible minimum version? What changes as duration, territories, partners, or resources increase? | Scale options and dependencies |

Do not immediately turn every proposal into a WP or application-form text. First compare at least one real alternative, including, where relevant, a smaller solution, a different action, or the continuation/improvement of an existing service.

## 0.3 Route discovery when no call is specified

If a call is already specified, verify its year, status, programme, action, sector, management, country/agency, applicant role, and current application form. If none is specified, create a shortlist without automatically choosing the most familiar action:

1. translate the idea, target, essential activities, geography, role, and scale into research requirements;
2. query the action catalogue and current official sources;
3. compare only routes genuinely open or relevant to the year/round under consideration;
4. verify objectives and eligible activities, applicants/partners/countries, participant profiles, duration, NA/EACEA management, funding model, and award criteria;
5. classify each route as `candidate`, `conditional`, `incompatible`, or `not-verified`, with source and reason;
6. justify the preferred route and preserve the best alternative.

A general page or thematic similarity is not enough. If changing sources cannot be verified, the selection remains provisional and cannot result in `PROCEED`. Erasmus+ and ESC may share the method, but not priorities, eligibility, rates, or scoring grids. Other EU programmes are candidate routes only if explicitly included in the mandate and verified against their sources; otherwise they remain context or possible complementarity.

## 0.4 Priority matrix

Assess each connection by **mechanism**, not by keywords. The matrix uses an explicit hierarchy:

| Level | Function | Rule of use |
|---|---|---|
| A — route/call | Action objectives, call priorities/topics, specific criteria and conditions | Determines applicability and design; cite source, version, and scope |
| B — applicable Erasmus+ | Horizontal or sectoral priorities and national provisions that genuinely apply | Connect need, target, activity, result, and evidence; do not assume universal applicability |
| C — contextual EU framework | European Youth Goals, EU Youth Strategy, and other consistent frameworks | Strengthens diagnosis and direction; does not create eligibility or points unless the route explicitly refers to it |
| D — complementarity | Priorities and instruments of other programmes or policies | Shows synergies/non-duplication; do not transfer rules, budgets, or criteria |

If the guide or an award criterion for the route explicitly requires European Youth Goals or another framework, that connection takes the official level of the source that requires it. Otherwise, it remains contextual. For each row, record: priority, level, source/locator, scope, linked need, concrete contribution, expected change, available evidence, risk of decorative alignment, and subsequent verification.

## 0.5 Primary and secondary targets

The **primary target** is the group in which the project seeks direct and measurable change. The **secondary target** is the group that enables, supports, multiplies, or indirectly benefits from the change. Communication audiences, stakeholders, and participants do not automatically coincide with either.

For each group, verify:

- the specific problem, need, and context, without automatically extrapolating EU data to the local level;
- compatibility with the route's expected profile, age, status, role, and countries;
- access, barriers, selection, support, safeguarding, and capacity to participate;
- role in co-design, implementation, and follow-up where required;
- realistic numbers and intensity in relation to activities, duration, and resources;
- expected outcome and a plausible way to observe it;
- relationship between primary and secondary targets, organisations, and communities.

A target incompatible with the action is a route or design problem, not a gap to fix with wording.

## 0.6 Tier, resources, and scale

Verify the actual funding scheme before discussing tiers. A tier exists only if the call source provides for one; otherwise compare scale scenarios without inventing official bands. For each option, consider:

- essential activities, number/intensity of participants, and geographic coverage;
- duration, number, and capacity of partners;
- plausible costs and funding model rules;
- coordination, monitoring, quality, and adoption complexity;
- strictly necessary outputs and realistically achievable outcomes;
- risk of oversizing or insufficient resources.

The decision does not follow from the highest available amount. The tier or scale must follow from the intervention logic and capacity, with a credible minimum version and a justification for each increase.

## 0.7 Multilevel causal chain

Formulate an initial chain together, always distinguishing data, hypotheses, and proposals:

`needs → problems/causes that can be addressed → solutions/mechanisms → activities → outputs → results/outcomes → contribution to impact`

The term `solutions` describes proposed strategies; activities implement them. Outputs are products or services delivered directly. `Results` are changes in use, skills, behaviours, practices, or conditions. Impact is broader and requires an adoption pathway and limits on attribution.

Analyse the relevant levels without forcing all of them:

| Level | Minimum check |
|---|---|
| Individual/participant | What direct change, for whom, and through what mechanism? |
| Organisation/professionals | What capacity, practice, or service changes, and who adopts it? |
| Community/territory | What diffusion of use, network, or condition can plausibly change? |
| Sector/system | What transfer, standards, policy learning, or collaboration is realistic? |
| European/transnational | Why does European cooperation or mobility add value compared with a local intervention? |

For each link, note evidence, assumption, external factor, next test, and alternative. Phase 0 requires a plausible chain, not a definitive ToC: research and `intervention-design` must test and detail it.

## 0.8 Strength assessment

Do not add up points. Use a reasoned assessment for each dimension. Award readiness is a design screening and does not replace the full `idea-validation` checklist:

- **route fit:** compatible objectives, activities, applicant, target, and geography;
- **preliminary award readiness:** a plausible project response exists for the route's main criteria, with gaps declared and no invented sub-scores;
- **relevance:** plausible need and substantive connection to applicable priorities;
- **causal coherence:** problem, solution, output, outcome, and impact are distinguishable and linked credibly;
- **European added value:** concrete reason for cooperation, mobility, or European scale;
- **feasibility:** plausible capacity, time, partnership, scale, and resources;
- **target and participation:** well-defined, reachable groups with a consistent role;
- **differentiation/gap:** existing response considered and contribution that avoids duplication;
- **verifiability:** explicit gaps with a plan, owner, and check point.

Use `strong`, `promising`, `fragile`, or `unsupported` as internal descriptors accompanied by evidence and consequence. Do not convert them into probabilities, official scores, or funding promises.

## 0.9 Decision

| Decision | When to use | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `PROCEED` | Route verified and compatible; plausible chain and targets; justified scale/tier; no hard blocker; remaining gaps can be addressed through research | Recommend handoff; initialise the project file only after an observed choice referring to the exact version |
| `REFINE` | The route remains plausible and the core idea can hold, but bounded changes or evidence are needed before the gate | Open a new revision of the same Idea Lab with a finite list of changes/research |
| `PIVOT` | The need may be valid, but the central route, target, solution, scale, or mechanism does not hold; a plausible alternative exists | Open a new revision of the same Idea Lab from the changed premises, without automatically reusing previous conclusions |
| `STOP` | A confirmed obstacle makes the idea unsuitable for application in the form considered and no credible pivot emerges, or the user decides not to proceed | Close the Idea Lab without creating a project file; record reason, evidence, and reopening conditions |

The table produces an **analytical recommendation**. The user's choice must be observed and bound to the exact `idea_version`, `selected_route`, and source snapshot/manifest. A change to any of the three requires a new revision and a new choice; do not reuse prior consent. A human choice does not make a route with a hard blocker compatible.

### Hard blockers

The following are blockers if confirmed by a source or reliable data:

- ineligible applicant, role, sector, countries, or target;
- essential activities or central objective outside the route's scope;
- call/round unavailable and no relevant alternative route;
- mandatory requirement incompatible with the project or not realistically satisfiable;
- scale/tier or funding model unable to support the core intervention;
- required role for recipients, safeguarding, or ethical conditions not feasible;
- chain with no plausible problem or credible mechanism, even after comparing alternatives;
- essential dependence on capacity, authorisations, or partners that are unavailable.

An unknown fact is a gap, not proof of a blocker. However, if it prevents identifying the route or verifying a decisive requirement, do not issue `PROCEED`: use `REFINE` with targeted research or `PIVOT` if a premise must change. `STOP` requires confirmed impossibility or a justified absence of alternatives, not merely weakness in the first draft.

## Output and handoff

`stages/phase-zero/decision.md` must contain at least:

1. brief and provenance of data;
2. options considered and alternative compared;
3. route shortlist or verification of the specified call;
4. multilevel priority matrix;
5. primary/secondary targets and preliminary compatibility;
6. tier/scale scenarios and feasibility;
7. multilevel causal chain with assumptions;
8. strength assessment by dimension;
9. hard blockers, gaps, and verification plan;
10. `PROCEED|REFINE|PIVOT|STOP` recommendation, observed choice, binding, rationale, and subsequent conditions/actions;
11. sources with version, scope, locators, and verification date.

With a `PROCEED` recommendation and an observed choice on the same exact package, the handoff to `idea-validation` includes the verified route and role, alternative, decision contract, sources, open questions, and owner. `idea-validation` performs the full preliminary eligibility assessment and [award coverage for the route](award-idea-checklists.md) on a frozen product; it does not inherit as proven any needs, priorities, or capacity described only as hypotheses in Phase 0. With `REFINE` or `PIVOT`, open a new revision of the Idea Lab and do not start the next gate. With `STOP`, preserve the closure without creating a fictitious application.
