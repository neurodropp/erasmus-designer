# Design methodology: from need to verifiable change

This is an internal engine procedure, proportionate to the programme, action, complexity and available data. Participatory diagnosis, gap analysis, trees, Theory of Change (ToC), logical framework, SMART/RACER and award criteria guide choices from project start through each individual answer. They do not constitute new upload requirements, a scoring grid or proof of effectiveness. Optional charts may be used; a table or structured text must still make the diagnosis, causal logic and traceability observable.

## Sources, authority and scope

The verified route determines the rules, criteria, weights and questions: the Programme Guide, call and amendments, provisions of the competent authority, current application form and relevant assessment guidance. Manage conflicts under the [evidence contract](../skills/erasmus-designer/references/evidence-contract.md).

The [Handbook on the lump sum funding model 2026, §1](https://www.erasmusplus.sk/erasmus_2021_2027/doc/vyzva/2026/Handbook_on_the_lump_sum_funding_model_2026.pdf#page=8) (printed p.6, PDF p.8) concerns KA2 Partnerships for Cooperation managed by National Agencies; the Programme Guide takes precedence. It is a cornerstone for KA220 and KA210, and for KA240 only on its relevant route. The specific sections for different partnerships remain distinct. [§2.2, p.11 PDF](https://www.erasmusplus.sk/erasmus_2021_2027/doc/vyzva/2026/Handbook_on_the_lump_sum_funding_model_2026.pdf#page=11) connects design, award criteria and questions; [Annex1, pp.33–34 PDF](https://www.erasmusplus.sk/erasmus_2021_2027/doc/vyzva/2026/Handbook_on_the_lump_sum_funding_model_2026.pdf#page=33) covers needs, gap analysis and SMART; [Annex2, p.35 PDF](https://www.erasmusplus.sk/erasmus_2021_2027/doc/vyzva/2026/Handbook_on_the_lump_sum_funding_model_2026.pdf#page=35) proposes RACER. These guidelines do not transfer WPs or lump sums to KA1, centrally managed actions or ESC. For KA210, maintain the simplified level for activities.

The Dutch National Agency's Impacttools, [Mobility KA1](https://www.erasmusplus.nl/en/impacttool-mobility) and [Partnerships KA2](https://www.erasmusplus.nl/en/impacttool-strategicpartnerships), support co-design and backward design: desired impact → outcome → output → activity → resources. They are methodological tools, not Italian provisions, additional official criteria or application form profiles. The concise 20-word assignment and the time horizons in their examples are exercises from the tool; they do not become limits or deadlines for the current form.

Explicit trees, a ToC with assumption testing, the logical framework matrix and the checks below are the engine's operational translation and must be distinguished from source requirements. For ESC, retrieve [its own route](../skills/esc-evidence-eligibility/SKILL.md); the method may be adapted, but these Erasmus+ sources do not establish ESC requirements or funding.

## Inputs and records

Open the file's [working memory](project-working-memory.md) and read the available brief, shared model, evidence, route and application form. Retain evidence, options, concise decisions and open questions; avoid chain of thought, simulated consultations and consent attributed without observation.

Before creating the file, when the idea or route has not yet stabilised, apply [Phase 0 — Idea Lab](phase-zero-idea-lab.md). Brainstorming remains divergent; convergence produces an observable decision contract with alternatives, sources, target, scale and recommendation `PROCEED|REFINE|PIVOT|STOP`. Only `PROCEED`, with a verified route, no hard blocker and an observed user choice on the exact binding, enables handoff to `idea-validation`; it does not replace its checklist, award coverage or independent validator.

Use the IDs from the [shared model](../wiki/templates/project-memory-contract.md) in all matrices. `project.json` retains shared data; research/design products explain choices and do not create alternative task, budget or calendar registers. The versioned product is `design/design-plan-vN.md` in the `intervention-design` phase (`design` in the compact process). `application/answer-plan-vN.md` retains the plan for the `answer-outlines` phase in the integrated process (`draft` in compact, before writing), including the criterion/question/instance matrix. Paths are relative to the project file. Pass the plans explicitly as step-products at the gate, with references to the model and evidence. The `award-coverage` matrix does not allocate official scores among fields. An accepted plan remains unchanged: a change creates a new version, invalidation of its owning phase and dependent checks.

The [method policy](../config/design-methodology.json) and [the eight methodological checks](../config/compliance/design-methodology-2026.json) maintain scope and application by phase; they are operational checks authorised by the user, not new call requirements. Apply them progressively: research delivers evidence/hypotheses, design closes the relevant logic, planning/writing select the links required by the fields, and review/QA verify products and frozen dependencies.

## 0. Idea Lab: from intuition to candidate route

Phase 0 explores the problem, target, mechanisms, alternatives and scale before committing the project to an application. If the call is not specified, compare real routes by year, action, sector, management, role, target, activities, funding scheme and required capacity. Do not automatically assign KA220, Erasmus+ or a sector based on the topic.

Build a priorities matrix that distinguishes:

- official objectives, priorities and criteria for the route/call;
- horizontal, sectoral and national Erasmus+ priorities that genuinely apply;
- EU contextual frameworks, such as European Youth Goals and EU Youth Strategy;
- complementarity with priorities from other programmes.

The contextual framework does not create requirements, eligibility or points unless expressly referenced in the applicable source. Each link must show the need, the concrete contribution and the expected change; matching keywords is not enough.

Distinguish primary targets, for which direct change is pursued, from secondary targets, which enable, multiply or receive indirect effects. Check their compatibility, access, role, numbers and outcomes against the route. Compare a credible minimum version with scale scenarios; refer to tiers only if the call provides for them.

Formulate the preliminary chain `needs → problems/causes → solutions/mechanisms → activities → outputs → results/outcomes → contribution to impact` at the relevant levels: individual, organisation, community, system and European. Each link retains its evidence or hypothesis, assumption/test and attribution limit. The [Phase 0 contract](../wiki/templates/phase-zero-decision-contract.md) governs the output; research and design deepen the chain without treating hypotheses as facts.

The recommendation is reasoned, without adding scores: `PROCEED` if the route and core are plausible; `REFINE` for limited changes or checks; `PIVOT` when a central premise must change but an alternative exists; `STOP` for a confirmed impediment with no credible pivot. An unknown is a gap, not proof of impossibility, but it prevents `PROCEED` when it blocks the route or a decisive requirement. The observed choice remains distinct and is tied to the exact idea version, route and source snapshot.

## 1. Participatory diagnosis and gap analysis

**Input:** real targets and contexts, primary/secondary data, partner capacity and existing interventions.

**Steps:** compare the current and desired situation by group/territory, distinguishing available measures from aspirations. Triangulate documentary evidence with the perspectives of target groups and partners; state the method, date, composition, limitations and disagreements. If participation has not taken place, provide a consultation plan and keep related claims unverified. Do not contact third parties without authorisation. Check what services/projects already offer and what gap remains: access, quality, capacity, transfer or another documented gap. Prioritise primary and secondary needs and possible effects, considering expected benefit, feasibility and equity.

**Output:** a current situation → desired situation → gap → evidence → stakeholder → need priority matrix, plus a data collection plan for gaps and a relevant alternative.

**Checks:** a programme priority does not prove a need; a European statistic is not a local baseline; a preference for a tool is not a diagnosis. For KA152, make young people's choices and tasks in design, preparation, implementation and follow-up observable, together with the intended learning.

**Stop:** insufficient evidence for the central need or exclusion of the target group's voice prevents the diagnosis from being declared validated; work may continue with conditional options.

## 2. Problem tree → objective tree

**Input:** diagnosis and priority gaps.

**Steps:** formulate the central problem as a verifiable negative condition; separate causes, problem and effects. For each link, state evidence, hypothesis or uncertainty; avoid circular reasoning and causes that merely repeat the desired solution. Identify what the partnership can control, influence or only observe. Turn negative conditions into desired conditions and cause/effect links into means/ends; review every transformation without assuming that rephrasing a statement proves causality. Select the branch that can be addressed and explain exclusions and alternatives.

**Output:** trees in table, text or graphic form, with links to need and objective IDs, evidence status and selected scope.

**Checks:** every objective responds to a gap and an addressable cause; broader effects remain distinct from the changes being pursued. No tree is classified as `required` without a call source.

**Stop:** a merely assumed central link or a cause beyond capacity requires research, reduced ambition or an alternative option; positive wording alone is insufficient.

## 3. ToC and intervention choice

**Input:** selected branch, evidence on interventions, partner mandate/capacity and plausible resources.

**Steps:** start from the desired lasting change and work backwards to outcomes, outputs, activities and resources. Explain the mechanism at every step: who uses what, which practice or condition changes and why. Then connect the chain forward to check its sufficiency. Record internal/external assumptions, available evidence, competing factors, risk of unintended effects, a relevant alternative and the basis for the choice. For decisive assumptions, define a signal/test, timing, owner and response if they do not hold.

**Output:** a verifiable ToC and concise assumptions → evidence/test → owner → decision register, with an adoption pathway and attribution limit.

**Checks:** outputs are products/services delivered directly; the model's `results` represent outcomes, meaning changes in use, behaviour, competence or practice; impact is the broader change to which the project contributes. Dissemination, downloads, participation and satisfaction may measure useful aspects, but do not automatically demonstrate outcomes or impact. Measuring contribution remains distinct from causal attribution to the project alone.

**Stop:** a leap from output to impact, implausible use or decisive assumption without a test requires substantial revision. A draft retains open dependencies without promising results.

## 4. Logical framework, SMART and RACER

**Input:** selected ToC and model updated by the coordinator.

**Steps:** build a matrix with level/ID, change or product, indicator, baseline, justified target, source/method, deadline, owner, assumptions/risks. Link activities and resources to results, including tasks/deliverables/WPs only in the structure relevant to the action. Estimate feasibility and costs iteratively: the initial estimate and scale inform objectives, while activity detail checks resources and the funding scheme.

**Output:** a logical framework and SMART/RACER checklist with an outcome and evidence or gap, not merely acronyms.

| Check | Observable question |
|---|---|
| SMART — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound | Are the target/change, measure or rubric, feasibility given capacity/resources, link to the need and verification timing clear? |
| RACER — Relevant | Does the indicator measure the stated level and objective? |
| RACER — Accepted | Is there an observed basis for agreement on the definition, method and responsibilities? If not, what validation is needed? |
| RACER — Credible | Is the measure understandable and verifiable by external observers as well? |
| RACER — Easy | Are collection and interpretation practicable given capacity, cost and a proportionate burden? |
| RACER — Robust | Do definitions, denominators, rubrics and checks reduce relevant ambiguity, manipulation, double counting and bias? |

**Checks:** distinguish baseline `unknown`, proven zero and justified NA. Numbers and targets must have a basis or remain proposed. Qualitative indicators with explicit criteria/rubrics are methodologically acceptable; do not impose an artificial percentage. Model checks verify structure and references, not causal effectiveness or partner agreement.

**Stop:** an unmeasurable objective or unsupported target remains to be validated; inconsistencies with resources, schedule or funding scheme require a model change before materialising consistent annexes.

## 5. Award criteria and individual answers

At initial validation, use [the route-specific checklist](award-idea-checklists.md), with variant/role where needed. `award_coverage` considers all subpoints through maturity, evidence and a gap plan; accepting the idea does not mean all final criteria are already met. Retain the accepted matrix as a step-product and dependency for research/design, without automatically choosing the first rubric for an action with multiple variants.

**Input:** official route criteria with locators, application form questions/instances and logical framework.

**Steps:** build a matrix of official criterion/subpoint → project choice/ID → evidence → question/instance → coverage/gap. Consider cross-cutting links: the same element may support multiple criteria and the same criterion may span multiple fields. Distinguish mapping attested by the source from the designer's interpretation; for administrative fields, use justified NA for assessment coverage while retaining the form obligations. Use criteria from the outset to choose and verify the need, objectives, architecture, partners, activities, results and impact.

**Output:** a coverage matrix and question-specific plans under the [response contract](../wiki/templates/response-contract.md). Each plan selects from the diagnosis/ToC/logical framework only what the field requires, with evidence and verified space for the question/instance pair. Do not automatically paste the internal map into the form.

**Checks:** textual coverage and design support are different; adding a sentence does not fix an ineffective activity. Do not invent sub-scores, per-answer weights or a cap taken from a methodological exercise. An unknown maximum remains unknown. Answers consistent with annexes retain the same IDs, targets and commitments.

**Stop:** an unverified grid permits a declared qualitative critique; unresolved form/limits prevent declaring an answer ready. Substantive gaps return to the design owner before rewriting.

## Handoff and review

The [design dossier](../wiki/templates/progettazione.md) makes these outputs readable without duplicating shared data. Research, design, planning, writing, KPI, WP, risk and follow-up owners receive the relevant artefacts, IDs and open questions. QA and the evaluator check the chain and its limitations against the frozen version; the [independent gate](orchestrator-protocol.md) remains distinct from methodological checks. A change to an accepted product requires invalidation of the first affected phase and new dependent checks; historical packages remain unchanged.
